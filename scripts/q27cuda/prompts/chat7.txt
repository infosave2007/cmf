The Danish energy island project in the North Sea was conceived as a hub that collects power from surrounding offshore wind farms and distributes it to several countries. Initially planned with a capacity of 3 GW and later 10 GW, the artificial island would sit roughly 80 km off the coast of Jutland. In 2023 the tender was postponed after cost estimates rose sharply, and the government asked for a redesign that could reduce the state's financial exposure. Alternatives discussed included platform-based solutions instead of a reclaimed island, phased construction, and closer integration with Belgian and German grids. Critics argued that delays put Denmark's 2030 climate targets at risk, while proponents said a cheaper design would still deliver the same electricity. Meanwhile, the Baltic Sea energy island around Bornholm proceeded on a different model, using the existing island as the hub, with connections to Zealand and Germany. Both projects depend on new HVDC converter technology and on the availability of specialised installation vessels, whose booking schedules have become a bottleneck across Europe.
